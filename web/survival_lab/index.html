    What does “relative hazard” mean? ＋

    The neural network outputs a Cox log hazard score. The displayed ratio compares it with one fixed reference: age 55, male survey category, BMI 27, seven hours sleep, 150 moderate recreational minutes, no vigorous minutes, 360 sitting minutes/day, never smoker, no reported diabetes or hypertension, self-rated health good. For example, 2× is twice that reference profile’s modeled instantaneous hazard, not a 2% death risk.

    Research prototype

    The Cox model was refitted so BMI is flat from 23–35 and has a positive fitted hazard slope above 35; the neural model is unchanged. Both long-range curves repeatedly reuse a model trained for short follow-up. The models were fitted with baseline ages 35–79, and the Cox fitting data ended below BMI 85. Inputs outside those ranges are zero-shot extrapolations, not validated predictions. A 50% crossing is not validated life expectancy, and changing an input does not establish a causal benefit.
