# Twin cosmetics (draft set)

Cosmetics for the rigged bear, drafted as vector layers that drop into the rig's clothing slots. Each item ships as:
- a vector file (V) per slot layer;
- a painted PNG (P) per layer, made with a gouache pass;
- an `item.json` describing the layers;
- a 120×120 tile thumbnail;
- composites on the rig, in the stand pose and a raised-arm pose.

Everything is regenerated by `tools/build.py`. The build reads `../rig/` and never writes to it. It is data-driven: arm variants, part boxes and pivots come from `rig.json` at build time, so the set follows the rig as it changes.

> **Rig state at the last build: v3.** Parts are sliced pixel-for-pixel from `sprites/v2/bear/nohat/stand.png`. The ears are painted into the head, and the arms are a single hanging piece pivoting at (126.5, 470) and (513.5, 470). The rig's big poses now swap whole v2 sprites, which carry no slots.
> - The composites therefore use fit-test poses owned by `tools/compose.py`: `stand`, `wave` (arm_l +62°), `stretch` (both arms ±70°) and `reach`.
> - An earlier rig had ear bones, an ear-tip redraw, and `up` / `think` / `hold` / `side` arm variants. The tools handled that rig as well: sleeves and wristband emit one file per arm variant, whatever variants exist.

## Head views (owner feedback: accessories must turn with the head)

Every item ships the **front view only**. Each `item.json` has a `views` block:
- `needed`:
  - hats, tops, scarf and aids need front, 3/4, side and back;
  - glasses need front, 3/4 and side, and draw nothing from behind.
- `available`: `["front"]`.
- `supported_poses`: the v2 poses whose head faces front: stand, wave, walk_front, sit, jump, stretch, think, eat, hold_ball, shrug and dizzy.
- `unsupported_poses`: walk_side (side), walk_back (back) and sleep (3/4 or lying).

**Rule:** on an unsupported pose, hide the item. Never paste a front-view accessory onto a turned head.
- The Cal hat's 3/4, side and back layers are being made separately under `sprites/v2/hat_layers/views/`. The logo-free `bear_hat_back.png` already exists. Wire them into `cal_hat` as they land.
- No view variants of the other cosmetics exist yet. That is background work.

## Hat fit (reworked after owner feedback)

Every hat is fitted to the original v2 head at the canonical Cal hat's scale.
- **Same frame as the canonical hat.**
  - The v2 hat compositor (`tools-v2/hatcomp/compose.py`) places the canonical layers at hat-stand px (8, 13). That is nohat px (5, 10), and rig px (61.5, 70), exactly where the rig puts its canonical hat.
  - Each hat's `item.json` `fit` block holds the frame measured from that hat:
    - `band_bottom`: the crown base every hat sits on, centre y 191;
    - `front_max_over_eyes`: the canonical lowest front edge (238 at centre, 244 over the eyes). Nothing may go below it over the eye columns, and the v2 eye tops are at y≈266;
    - `anchor`: (320, 191).
- **Ears rise over the hat. No slots are cut in the crown.** The crown sits between the ears and slightly behind them.
  - Each crowned hat has an `ear_over` layer (head bone, z72). It holds the ears, cut from the rig's head part along a soft round arc: the ear's cupped base, about 22 px below the ear centre, feathered 1.3 px. It is drawn over `hat_front`.
  - Under each arc the crown gets a soft contact shadow.
  - The beanie and cap crowns sit lower between the ears (crown top about 93, just above the head dome at about 100). Their cuff and band follow the head curve.
  - Our hats keep the plain `head` variant. `cal_hat` uses `hatclip`, like the rig does for the canonical hat.
  - The visor has no crown, so it needs no ear layer.
- **Checked side by side.** `hat-fit-sheet.png` shows each hat in six panels:
  1. on the v2 stand;
  2. the v2 canonical (`hat/stand.png`);
  3. on the rig;
  4. the rig canonical;
  5. outline overlay: this hat in red, the canonical in grey, plus the head and v2 eyes;
  6. on a 1.15× head.
  - Every hat now sits snugly on the crown, leaves the eyes clear on both bodies, keeps the ears out, and stays within the canonical width. The only exception is the straw sun hat's brim, which is a little wider by design (58–582 vs 62–571).
- **Per-head scaling for age and build variants.** Hats ride the head bone, so a scaled head carries the hat (panel 6).
  - For a new head shape, scale by `head_width / 396` about `anchor`, then move the anchor to that head's crown-base centre.
  - Re-derive `front_max_over_eyes` from the new eye line. A variant with a different ear layout needs its own ear circles for `ear_over`.
- **v2 flat sprites.** To convert rig px to v2 hat-stand px, use x = (x−49)/2.5 + 3 and y = (y−45)/2.5 + 3. Then apply `hatc/bear_match.json` per pose, exactly as the canonical hat is composited.

## Slot contract (`item.json`, schema `lifetwin.cosmetic.v1`)

```json
{ "id": "cal_tee", "category": "top",
  "layers": [ { "name": "sleeve_l", "slot": "top_sleeve_l", "bone": "arm_l", "z": 35.15, "pivot": [180, 474],
                "box": {"x": 92, "y": 404, "w": 168, "h": 190},
                "vector": "sleeve_l.default.svg", "painted": "sleeve_l.default.p.png",
                "variant": "default", "follow": "arm_l" }, ... ],
  "colourable": { "body": {"key": "#24426f", "tones": {"hi": "...", "lt": "...", "base": "...", "sh": "...", "dk": "...", "ink": "..."}} },
  "hide_body_slots": [], "material": "jersey", "notes": [...] }
```

- **Coordinates.** Every SVG path is in absolute rig px, on the same 640 × 900 canvas as `rig.json` with the anchor at (320, 852). Each file's `viewBox` equals its `box`.
  - To place a layer, draw it into `Rect(box)` in rest-pose rig space, inside the bone's transform. That is the same thing the rig does for a slot variant's `x/y/w/h`.
  - The painted PNG covers the same box at 2× resolution.
- **Bone.** Rotate and translate the layer with its bone, about the bone's `pivot`, just like the body part in the same slot group.
- **`variant` / `follow`.** Limb-borne layers have one file per arm variant (`default`, `up`, `think`, `hold`, `side`). Show the file whose `variant` equals the current variant of the `follow` slot.
  - When the arm swaps to `up`, the sleeve swaps to `sleeve_l.up.svg`.
  - If a layer has no file for the current variant, hide it. The cane, for example, only exists on `default`.
- **Painted vs vector.** V files use only paths, gradients and `clipPath`, with no filters, masks or CSS. `flutter_svg` can render them.
  - P files are pre-baked PNGs. Use them where painted fidelity matters, such as the Customize stage. Use V where crisp scaling matters.

### Z-order for Flutter (inside each bone group, ascending; the rig's own numbers)

```
legs 10 · torso 30 · top 32 · scarf_tail 33 · cane 34.9 · arm 35 · top_sleeve 35.15 · wrist band 35.25
· scarf_wrap 36 · hat_under 45 · aid_back 49 · head (ears painted in) 50 · aid_tube 55 · fur_patches 55
· fur_blush 56 · eyes/eyelids 60 · glasses 65 · hat_front 70 · ear_over 72
```

The bone hierarchy decides which groups nest. For example, `neck` and `head` are children of `torso`, so the whole head draws after the torso's own children with z below its subtree minimum. The cosmetic layers follow that same rule.

### Recolouring (colourable regions)

Each region lists six hexes: `hi`, `lt`, `base`, `sh`, `dk` and `ink`. All six come from one key colour via `tools/paint.py:tones()`:

```
hi  = mix(key, #fff3df, .46)    lt = mix(key, #ffeed6, .24)    base = key
sh  = mix(key, #2b2148, .28)    dk = mix(key, #1c1430, .48)
ink = mix(mix(key, #140c18, .56), #3b2418, .25)          (mix = linear RGB blend)
```

To recolour, compute the six hexes for the new key and string-replace them in the V file. For the painted version, re-bake the P PNG: run `python tools/build.py --only <id>` after changing the key.

Fixed details are not colourable. These include the brass collar, ferrule, lens tints and the tracker face.

### Fur palettes

`fur/<id>/item.json` gives each part group (`head`, `torso`, `tail`, `ear`, `arm`, `leg`) a per-channel, monotone, piecewise-linear map. It sends honey's anchors (outline, fur dark/mid/light, belly, belly light) to the palette's anchors.
- The map is monotone, so every in-between value keeps its place on the ramp, and the painted shading and fur strokes survive. The tables are `tables_33`: 33 samples per channel in 0..1.
- Panda uses a white map on the head and torso and a black map on the arms and legs, plus the `fur_patches` eye-patch overlay. The older rig also had separate ear and tail parts; v3 paints the ears into the head, so v3 ears go white with the head.
- Face parts and `chin_shadow` are not recoloured.
- The head part contains the painted cheeks, so the map would wash them out. Every non-honey palette adds `fur_blush`.
- **Flutter:**
  - P body parts: bake once per palette with `tools/fur.py:bake(pid, out_dir)`, or apply `tables_33` in a fragment shader.
  - V parts: map every fill hex through the same tables.
- **Web:** `filter.svg` holds the same maps as `feComponentTransfer` filters. `preview.html` uses them.

### Human overlays (secondary)

The human has no rig yet. `human/<id>/*.svg` re-emits the hats and glasses as flat overlays in the pixel space of `sprites/v2/human/nohat/stand.png` (198 × 395).
- The viewBox is padded by 40 px so wide brims fit. `item.json` holds `transform_from_rig`.
- Draw `hat_under` behind the sprite and every other layer over it.
- The `ear_over` layer, the ear contact shadow and the bear-face brim shadow are left out (the human has no bear ears).
- The fit is two fitted affines, one for hats (band + hair width) and one for glasses (eye spacing). This is a draft until the human gets the shared skeleton.

## How it is drawn

- **One light, upper-left, for everything.** Each form has an off-centre radial base, edge pooling on the shadow side, a soft key-light glow, dry-brush marks along the form, and a warm ink outline.
  - Silhouette lines are 2.4 rig px and seams 1.7 rig px. The ink is pulled toward the bear's outline brown.
  - Cast shadows are tinted and soft (radial falloff), never grey: brim on the face, chin on the chest, frames on the fur, the cane at the ground.
- **Fit comes from the rig's own masks.**
  - Tops dilate the torso mask by 4–5 px, so the cloth stands off the fur.
  - Sleeves dilate each arm-variant mask and union in a disc around the shoulder socket pivot. The disc doesn't change when the arm rotates, so the joint stays covered at every angle (no crack at the shoulder). Long sleeves stop on an arc around the paw; short sleeves stop on an arc around the socket.
  - The knit scarf reuses the canonical scarf silhouettes.
  - The visor band is clipped to the head silhouette, so it wraps around.
- **Ears over hats.** See Hat fit: each crowned hat has an `ear_over` layer that redraws the ears above the crown with a soft round base.
- **P pass** (`tools/painted.py`). The V layer is rendered at 2×, then:
  - painted material detail is added from `textures/swatches.png`: twill, knit, straw, jersey, cable, fleece, nylon, woven band and wood;
  - a brush-dab field is laid along each item's flow direction, with paper tooth and mottling;
  - pigment pools at the edges, and vector edges are softened slightly.
  - Colours stay the V colours.
- **Swatch source.** `textures/swatches.png` was generated once through the local Codex CLI (`cx auto codex exec -m gpt-6-sol`, image tool). The approved painted parts were the style references: `hat_hd_front.png`, `scarf_wrap.png` and `torso.png`. No API keys were used.

## Rebuild

```
python tools/build.py                 # everything (~60 s): layers, P bake, thumbs, previews, sheet, human, preview.html
python tools/fitcheck.py              # hat fit sheet (v2 stand + rig vs the canonical hat, bigger head)
python tools/build.py --only cap,hoodie --no-preview
```

This needs Python 3.10+, numpy, scipy, Pillow, `resvg_py` and `vtracer` (the scratchpad venv has them).
