# Cal Hacks idea audit — 23 October 2026

## Recommendation

**Build RecallDesk: the recall closeout desk for a small secondhand store with separate payment/POS and consignor systems.** Its core output is an evidence-backed handoff into the existing financial system: which units are held, whose payout needs review, which remedies might apply, and what money actually arrived. Start with a synthetic independent resale shop, two real recall notices, and exported inventory/sales records. The proposed finance seats are **inventory exception analyst + recovery reconciliation clerk**. This is the best candidate in this audit for the finance rubric and a memorable live failure-recovery demonstration. It is a recommendation under uncertainty, not a prediction of winning.

The opening moment: the system rejects a reassuring “$1,000 recovered” figure because the official recall offers replacements rather than cash. It also holds a matching power-bank model for serial verification instead of assuming that every unit qualifies. The owner sees stock and financial consequences change together; the evidence behind every change is one click away. Inject a duplicate bank callback and recovered cash stays unchanged.

**Demand is established for the underlying screening/exception workflow, not for this exact product.** An independent consignment shop publicly says it checks recalls; a nonprofit thrift operator documents rejecting categories because it cannot resolve recall/expiry uncertainty; a scaled recommerce operator documents screening and remediation. Those are stronger demand signals than a pitch or a regulator warning alone. The finance-closeout gap, buyer budget, time saved, and willingness to pay remain hypotheses. No interviews, purchases, pilot commitments, or paid conversions were fabricated or conducted.

The strongest objection: cheap recall tools already match inventory, explain evidence, hide listings, and export records, while consignment POS products already handle payouts, returns and ownership states. RecallDesk only earns a new claim if it completes the **recall-evidence-driven handoff between those existing systems** better than their composition. A beautiful scanner with a replacement ledger should be killed. If no representative closeout artifact can be obtained legitimately before the build commitment, narrow to an explicit simulation or select the simpler shopping backup below; do not imply real retailer integration.

**Shopping backup: ShelfLife**, a supported-lifetime shopping decision for a buyer who needs a refurbished Chromebook for a specific course or work horizon. It compares the actual listing to authoritative model support dates and necessary application features, with all-in cost and uncertainty. This is easier to finish, but AUE-aware shopping comparisons already exist. Its defensible distinction would be resolving a particular ambiguous listing and refusing a false bargain under the buyer's constraints—not discovering the concept of update expiry.

## 1. RecallDesk — deep audit

### Demand: what people actually do

- **Independent operator:** [The Elephant's Trunk standards](https://www.theelephantstrunk4kids.com/our-standards) explicitly describe checking consignments for recalls and requiring manufacturer identification. This establishes that the work is performed by a specific small resale operator; it supplies no time or cost measurement.
- **Real financial handoff inputs:** the same operator's [public consignment agreement](https://www.theelephantstrunk4kids.com/consignment-agreement) documents a 40% consignor share after fees, a 120-day consignment period with ownership transfer for unsold goods, payout availability 7–14 days after sale for exchanges, and multiple payout modes. This establishes real ownership/payout bookkeeping alongside screening. The agreement does **not** specify recall-specific clawbacks or grant us authority to deduct consignor funds; those must remain unresolved until verified.
- **Recall, ownership and refunds in one artifact:** [Bushel & Peck's agreement](https://shopbushelandpeck.com/pages/consignor-guide-agreement) combines recall-triggered removal, consignor ownership until disposition, commission terms, and approved-refund reversal of previously paid commissions. It also distinguishes a store-credit bonus from cash. This directly substantiates an ownership/payout reconciliation workflow; its frequency, current software and automation budget are still unknown. The [JBF Sugar Land January 2023 agreement](https://sugarland.jbfsale.com/Valet%20Consignor%20Agreement%20and%20Liability%20Waiver%20-1-.pdf) historically links defect refunds to nonpayment of consignors. Neither document authorizes our prototype to apply its terms to another shop.
- **Documented cross-system workaround:** [Consignor Connect's Rose refund help](https://help.consignorconnect.com/knowledge/how-to-refund-in-rose) says a Square refund requires separate manual changes to Rose sales/payout records; an already-paid consignor requires a manually recorded charge. This is direct evidence that a real tool workflow has a handoff burden. It establishes neither how often recalls cause it nor whether an existing integration/customization solves it for a given shop.
- **Costly workaround:** [Rockford Rescue Mission's donation FAQ](https://old.rockfordrescuemission.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Thrift-Store-Donations-FAQ_revised11-2021.pdf) describes declining baby equipment because recall/expiry uncertainty exceeds its ability to determine safety, and describes disposal as diverting resources from its programs. The document is older and proves a concrete policy, not current market prevalence. Do not use the prototype to override a store's category bans.
- **Scaled operation:** [GoodBuy Gear's safety process](https://goodbuygear.com/pages/safety) describes recall screening before resale and physical inspection. Its [operator explanation](https://goodbuygear.com/blogs/expert-insights/is-good-buy-gear-legit) says affected goods can be removed/remedied and past customers notified. This establishes an operational benchmark and also competition; it is a company claim, not an independent audit.
- **Government basis:** [CPSC reseller guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/ResaleThrift-Stores) explicitly includes thrift shops, consignment, charities and casual resale. A recall screen does not establish overall product safety, and a negative search is not a resale clearance.

Inference: small stores with these documented screening and payout obligations may benefit from a reliable handoff into disposition and finance work. Unvalidated: how frequently that handoff produces material financial work, whether a store would buy a separate tool, and whether existing POS/accounting software already suffices. Consumer recall remedies do not automatically establish a reseller's commercial entitlement.

### Accessible data: actual fetch, not just documentation

[CPSC API documentation](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/CPSC-Recalls-Application-Program-Interface-API-Information) describes public XML/JSON recall access. A live request was actually executed during this audit:

`https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Cosori`

The saved [raw response](fixtures/cpsc-live-fetch.json) contains one official notice, recall 23-130. This is a **cached result of a successful live fetch**, not a permanently live service. An initial attempt with `RecallNumber=25117` returned an empty array; empty results must be distinguished from transport failure.

Observed schema problem: `Products[0].Model` is empty and `ProductUPCs` is empty, while exact models appear in `Description`. A simplistic structured-field join fails on this real record. The [official Cosori notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2023/Two-Million-COSORI-Air-Fryers-Recalled-by-Atekcity-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards) offers a replacement remedy; it does not establish a cash refund for our invented store. An [official Anker notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2025/Anker-Power-Banks-Recalled-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards-Manufactured-by-Anker-Innovations-1) requires serial verification and evidence. Manufacturer eligibility lookup is a separate dependency and was **not** executed. No serials, registrations, contacts, or claims were submitted.

What must be synthetic for the weekend without an authorized partner: store stock, invoice/ownership records, consignor terms, customers, claim approvals and bank events. Clearly label every one. What can be real: official notices, actual public source retrieval, extracted model/remedy evidence, timestamps, code execution. Public recall text does not supply business refund liability, ownership rights, accounting policy or claim acceptance.

### Smallest credible build

One CSV of 12 household-product units; two official notices; one store policy document explicitly marked synthetic. A notice-ingestion worker uses a useful public-web collection integration if credentials are available. A model extracts candidate rules with quoted spans; deterministic code applies only accepted rules and controls money-like states. The app shows four lanes: **held stock / proof required / remedy work / financially reconciled**. A physical shelf illustration or product-tile grid makes each action visible. The right panel shows the originating sentence and a timeline of allowed actions.

The agent autonomously reads and drafts, classifies evidence, marks a **local simulated** inventory hold, prepares proof requests and reconciles test events. It does not auto-submit claims, send notifications, approve new bank details, issue refunds, dispose of products or publish a safety clearance. Those actions are outside this research authorization and would need product-specific operating authority. Do not spend the weekend integrating five marketplaces.

Finance demonstration: show gross at-risk stock, documented customer-refund obligation, pending remedy, replacement value and actual cash received separately. Initial proposed entries are workpapers, not official accounting postings. Owner/eligibility uncertainty remains visible rather than manufactured into a receivable. A two-minute flow completes with a reproducible packet and a held-out event.

### Experiments completed and next kill tests

**Completed:** live public API feasibility fetch; raw-schema inspection; manual review of exact competitor features; executable local financial-state fixture. Final [results](fixtures/policy-results.json): **20/20** assertions passed, including replacement versus cash, serial uncertainty, eligibility uncertainty, partial settlement, duplicates and invalid amounts. Four final handoff cases use a synthetic 50/50 full-return policy: only propose the consignor's share, never invent clawback rights from missing terms, distinguish reversing an unpaid commission from cash recovery, and preserve the recall hold through return processing. These are proposed workpapers, not actual deductions or vendor integrations. The fixture's naive recovered-value ablation would count $100 replacement value as $100 cash; the corrected policy returns $0 spendable cash. The first script run exposed the system Python's pre-3.10 annotation behavior; adding postponed annotations fixed execution. These are software observations, not LLM accuracy, accounting compliance, legal advice, user demand or product effectiveness measurements.

**Unrun, useful next:** obtain an authorized store operator's last three recall exceptions, including one completed disposition and a financial artifact. Time their current work versus a blinded packet **and the same job using their existing POS plus a current recall scanner**. Keep the wedge only if at least two artifacts require cross-system ownership/remedy/settlement work and the operator finds the packet correct and materially easier to use. If the existing composition does the handoff equally well, or the shop has no repeated handoff burden, kill the claimed advantage. For adoption, ask them to bring the next real exception into a bounded pilot; a hypothetical yes is weaker evidence. Do not claim this threshold has been met.

**90-minute first-build gate:** fetch one real notice, accept one exported or explicitly synthetic unit record, expose missing identifiers and remedy terms, then produce one proposed handoff linking a source span to the exact inventory/finance fields that need review. The gate passes only if the record can carry a recall hold through a normal return event while keeping replacement value separate from cash. Do not spend these 90 minutes polishing a landing page.

**Abandonment threshold:** at the three-hour checkpoint, if the prototype still ends at “recall detected,” cannot ingest the intended store export without invented fields, or cannot produce a traceable handoff beyond an existing POS-plus-scanner flow, abandon the cross-system finance claim. Switch to a smaller explicit policy simulation or another chosen candidate. A synthetic fixture can prove the prototype's state logic; it cannot rescue a claim of a working real-store integration.

## 2. Grant Runway — deep audit

**User and job:** a small housing-services nonprofit finance manager receives a delayed reimbursement or restricted award; the agent constructs a feasible schedule of already-approved commitments, flags unspendable balances, drafts the reimbursement/approval evidence packet, and explains which service commitments can still be met. The differentiator to investigate is **cash timing under award-specific constraints**, not grant discovery or ordinary tagging.

**Problem evidence:** the [Urban Institute's nationally representative 2025 survey report](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/how-government-funding-disruptions-affected-nonprofits-early-2025) found a third of nonprofits reported government funding disruption. [Nonprofit Finance Fund's 2025 survey](https://nff.org/wp-content/uploads/NFF-2025-Survey-Report-1.pdf) reports late payment among a majority of respondents receiving government funding; its sample is not a population estimate. These establish pain and cash-management work, not demand for our automation or causation from software gaps.

**Direct competition:** [Ramp Grants Hub](https://support.ramp.com/set-up-grants-hub) supports grant-coded spending, budgets, award letters, grant managers and review routing. [Sage Intacct grant capabilities](https://www.sage.com/en-us/sage-business-cloud/intacct/product-capabilities/extended-capabilities/grants-tracking-billing/) include reimbursable expenses and grant tracking/billing; [purpose-based restriction release](https://www-p107.intacct.com/ia/docs/en_US/help_action/General_Ledger/Nonprofit_restriction_release/about-restriction-release.htm) already automates release calculations. These discoveries directly invalidate “first AI grant-aware purchasing/accounting platform.” An autonomous counterfactual cash plan could still differ, but the reviewed pages do not establish that such planning is absent from the market.

**Accessible inputs:** public sample awards and federal cost principles can ground a demo; real nonprofit bank, payroll, award amendments, allowable-cost evidence and approval policies require an authorized partner. Use synthetic signed award excerpts with explicit boundaries, not a claim that arbitrary PDFs can be interpreted reliably. The applicable [current official allocable-cost rule](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.405), checked at the eCFR version current to 21 October 2026, must be considered for the exact program; a numerical optimizer must not move costs between awards simply to fill a shortfall.

**Weekend slice:** three funds, eight commitments, two reimbursement dates, one timing shock. The model extracts proposed constraints with spans; a small solver computes feasible plans; the agent drafts one approval/reimbursement packet. Visual: three transparent tanks of money with labeled restrictions, then a timeline of service commitments. Moment: a large bank balance still cannot pay unrestricted payroll; the system provides a feasible partial plan and shows which assumption prevents the rest. A slider changes arrival date without rewriting history.

**Stress cases:** award end versus service date; invoice paid after period; purpose versus time restrictions; shared costs and documented allocation basis; indirect-cost caps; reimbursement approved but not received; award amendment supersedes old term; cash shortage cannot be “solved” by invalid allocation; duplicate invoice; restricted funds commingled in one bank account; unsupported assumption on match funding; missing labor evidence. Each should yield a stated constraint or review state, not an invented exception. Restricted net assets, award budgets, receivables and available cash are different concepts; the demo must state its simplified accounting model.

**Kill criteria:** if the core product is grant tagging, budget review, letter extraction, or approval routing, incumbent features already cover it. If a representative finance operator cannot identify a recurring planning decision absent from current tools, do not promise an underserved market. No primary user interview or solver benchmark was executed for this candidate. A suitable next test uses one de-identified actual planning artifact and asks an operator to challenge the plan with a late-reimbursement scenario. Do not describe synthetic solver success as a dollar of recovered funding.

**Why second:** strongest observed pain category and excellent sponsor fit, but award-specific interpretation and close incumbent overlap increase the burden of proving a meaningful wedge. RecallDesk has a cleaner public trigger and easier-to-understand adversarial moment. If the team has nonprofit domain access and real award documents, this ranking can reverse.

## 3. ShelfLife — deep audit

**User and job:** a student buying a refurbished Chromebook needs it through a two-year program and needs a specific application feature. The system checks the exact model, support window, extended-update caveats and seller evidence before showing a ranked supported basket. It can prepare a comparison and purchase handoff; it cannot guarantee hardware longevity or invent unlisted battery condition.

**Primary evidence:** [Google's Auto Update policy](https://support.google.com/chrome/a/answer/6220366?hl=en) publishes model support dates, ties the period to platform release and specifically tells buyers to check dates before purchasing. [Extended-update documentation](https://support.google.com/chromebook/answer/14514725?hl=en) describes opt-in models and feature caveats. These establish a real constraint; “every new purchase gets ten more years” is false. [Google's education repair/reuse description](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/earth-day-2024/) documents refurbishment as an actual purchasing pathway. [Back Market's Chromebook category](https://www.backmarket.com/en-us/l/chromebook-laptop/1c5f4bcd-4d1c-4f29-8a47-de7c52e89565) shows current refurb supply. None establishes how many buyers make this mistake or pay for a prevention tool.

**Competition found:** [So geht Chromebook's finder](https://so-geht-chromebook.de/chromebook-finder/) filters models using update end, price and hardware attributes, and its [comparisons](https://so-geht-chromebook.de/chromebooks/vergleich/) already pair price with update dates. [wolaptop](https://wolaptop.de/en/vergleich/chromebook) compares new/used offers with AUE discussion. [Edventory's AUE report](https://edventory.com/tools/chromebook-aue-report) offers uploaded fleet expiry grouping and replacement budgets. Back Market also [flags ageing MacBook software support](https://help.backmarket.com/hc/en-us/articles/27149752626972-What-is-software-obsolescence-and-what-does-it-mean-for-my-MacBook). Thus expiry-aware shopping and fleet planning are established, not new. Pricing/affiliate incentives and language/geography may leave opportunities but do not prove an unserved segment.

**Proposed narrower distinction:** resolve an arbitrary actual used listing's ambiguous exact model and return **verified / missing evidence / conflicts**, including what a buyer should ask the seller before spending. Rank by the buyer's minimum horizon and required features, not simply an average-supported-month number. Do not mark a listing ineligible solely because the model family name is vague; ask for the label or exact code. A nominally long AUE may not preserve an application under extended updates.

**Data and scope:** Google's model table is public; public retailer listings can be fetched with an available web-data integration subject to access rules. Start with one retailer and six listings. The buyer enters the needed date and feature; no consumer purchase history is necessary. Item price may be real while tax/shipping remain estimated or unknown; show that distinction. API/search availability for listing extraction must be tested before choosing a particular merchant. This audit did not run a live listing extraction or resolve a representative listing automatically.

**Weekend slice:** paste one real listing link, extract evidence, show a product with a visible time bar ending before the user's course ends, then propose two actual alternatives. The user can change the course horizon and see re-ranking with reasons. Visual: a cash receipt unrolls into a support timeline; unresolved model identity stays visibly unresolved. Add checkout only if sponsor docs and a legitimate sandbox permit it; a comparison alone does not prove payment integration.

**Stress cases:** device/manufacture date confused with platform launch; model-family variants; out-of-date source; extended updates remove required Android functionality; unsupported claim of OS migration; seller's title conflicts with label; locked enterprise enrollment; battery health absent; US listing versus different shipping country; course requirement is Windows-only; minimum horizon fails by one month; shipping pushes budget over limit. The system should distinguish a support-date fact from assumptions about physical life, device condition, and third-party software availability.

**Next experiment / kill test:** sample 20 public listings and have a human label the exact-model/support evidence; hide five until extraction/rule logic is frozen. Measure **correct abstention** and supported recommendation rate, not just answer rate. Then, if authorized, observe five people selecting a laptop for a real requirement with and without the tool; record decisions and false confidence. Exact product WTP is unvalidated and a free public tool may be the right model. Kill the novelty claim if the final demo is only a price/AUE table.

**Why third:** easiest complete shopping flow and strongest consumer readability, but existing comparison tools reduce distinction and evidence of buyer behavior is weaker than the resale operators' own procedures. It can be a better choice for a team with little finance experience.

## Adjacent ideas we deliberately did not crown

**Door-to-Door:** [Room & Board](https://www.roomandboard.com/customer-service/measuring-for-delivery) explains the whole-route measurement job and offers Fit Calls, showing a real retail workflow. But [Will It Fit](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/will-it-fit-ar-viewer/id6790808866), [Scooch](https://getscooch.com/) and [Poché](https://getpoche.com/tools/will-it-fit) already address size/doorways/route fit in varying forms. Poché explicitly connects floor plans and shopping. A robust motion-planning witness is technically interesting but a whole-home guarantee is a poor weekend promise. A 2D fixture cannot establish 3D delivery safety.

**Repair Cart:** [PartSelect's own help](https://www.partselect.com/help/) already uses model-specific diagrams and part lookup; its [serial/series documentation](https://www.partselect.com/Help) explains mid-model changes. [iFixit FixBot](https://www.ifixit.com/News/114700/introducing-fixbot) explicitly supports model identification, compatibility-checked part discovery, repair guidance, voice and label photos; [FixBot Pro](https://www.ifixit.com/Info/FixBot_Pro) targets professional repair operations. This was a decisive negative finding. Multi-vendor landed-cost procurement might remain a wedge, but the basic proposal should not be pitched as novel.

**CloseLab:** [Numeric](https://www.numeric.io/) positions agents, reporting, close and cash management together; [FloQast's own newsroom](https://www.floqast.com/press) reports auditable agents and commercial scale. A simulator could target training/evaluation rather than do the close, but demand for that separate product was not established here. Synthetic financial data is feasible; real book access remains missing.

**Lienline:** [Siteline](https://www.siteline.com/) and its [FAQ](https://www.siteline.com/faq) directly cover pay applications, lien waivers, compliance records and ERP-adjacent work. The domain has genuine high-value operational work but a generic “agent handles pay apps” proposition is directly crowded. A narrow changed-bank/fraud trap or lien waiver checker still needs state-specific boundaries and representative documents.

**PalletProof:** [Liquidation.com's manifest guide](https://www.liquidation.com/c/ReadingAManifest) documents a spreadsheet-based buying workflow, inconsistent manifest fields and quantity variances.
