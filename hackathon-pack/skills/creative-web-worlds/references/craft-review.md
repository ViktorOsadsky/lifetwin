# Review for craft

Use this as a critique lens, not a points checklist that can certify subjective quality.

## Freeze-frame review

- **Concept:** Could content and logo be swapped into another industry without affecting the composition? Name what should be more specific.
- **Hierarchy:** Is there a clear first, second and third read? Does one element carry authority while supporting elements have deliberate scale and space?
- **Assets:** Do silhouette, crop, detail, material, light and edge quality hold up at display size? Is a weak asset hidden behind effects or giant type?
- **Typography:** Is the face appropriate? Are line breaks, tracking, baseline relationships and optical alignment authored? Is small text readable rather than random microtype?
- **Composition:** Are asymmetry and overlap controlled relationships? Is empty space active, or missing content? Inspect the whole page, not only the first viewport.
- **Details:** Do controls, icons, dividers, surfaces and captions use a coherent grammar? Are arbitrary numbers and decorative brackets compensating for an underdeveloped idea?
- **Content:** Does the copy sound like this particular institution/product/person? Remove vague claims and fabricated prestige. Let real content shape layout.

## Experience review

Inspect opening, resting, interacting, changed, blocked and ending states. A strong opening must resolve into a useful page. A button must do the thing it names; a scene should respond with continuity. Avoid permanent camera motion, noisy filters and hover-only discoveries. Design mobile deliberately rather than shrinking desktop into a pile of cards.

Use measurements for measurable things: overflow, targets, contrast, latency, draw calls, resource size. Use specific comparative critique for aesthetics: shallow lighting erases a silhouette is useful; 9.7/10, looks premium is not.

## Revision record

Save prompt/brief, source relationships, rendered screenshot, actual criticism, change and rerun. Separate self-review, independent agent review and user verdict. Do not erase an unfavorable review by averaging it into a high score. If no independent review occurred, say so.

Before treating a full-page capture as evidence, scroll through image-led sections and confirm the intended assets have rendered. A capture can contain unloaded images without overflow or console errors. Inspect important regions at their actual display size; a long-page thumbnail can hide missing imagery, tiny controls, and weak text contrast.

Compare experiments with one another as well as with references. Different subject matter and rendering technology can still reproduce one house style: the same oversized wordmark, pale/dark chapters, small perimeter labels, arrow links and giant farewell. To test broader generalization, choose a subsequent brief whose content and task require a different hierarchy, type voice and page rhythm. Do not impose those alternatives on a brief that explicitly asks for the existing style.

## What the first experiment taught

Pocket Market combined generic system typography, flat primitives and cosmetic rotations with a working basket. Its tests established implementation behavior, not ambitious art direction. The user rejected its appearance. The corrective lesson is asset/typographic/composition production and rendered comparative review—not a universal ban on SVG, playful work or market metaphors.
