# Illustrated pets, cozy games and tactile controls

Build an original visual world. Talking Tom can inform character prominence and care response; do not reproduce its character, branding or assets. SVG-esque describes clean authored shapes and expression, not a requirement for one file format or a reason to use generic clip art.

## Author the character and its place, when present

Choose the creature's silhouette, personality, pose and recognizable feature together. Test its silhouette and face at final phone size before adding particles or HUD. Use intentional curves, limited shape families, coherent line weight, expressive eye/mouth spacing and believable attachments. Layered circles can be a sketch, but extra shading cannot rescue an unresolved silhouette or pasted-on features. Original asymmetry, pose and a small narrative prop can carry personality without more copy.

Compose a habitat with foreground/midground/background, a clear resting or contact plane, and a focal light direction. The character should inhabit it rather than float on a gradient behind rounded cards. A quiet painterly room, cut-paper woodland, toy diorama and crisp graphic aquarium demand different material choices; do not turn every cute brief into glossy pastel clay. Let decorative detail support the main silhouette. Inspect contour collisions with background props; choose clear separation or unmistakable overlap so a limb or ear does not merge into the scenery. Reserve contrast and saturation for the character, relevant needs and useful actions.

Treat art and controls as related layers, not identical objects. Use a consistent family of icon stroke, bevel/edge, radius and shadow rules; status chips, primary action buttons and world props may differ in shape and depth to establish hierarchy. A polished icon is a small authored drawing, not an emoji whose appearance changes across platforms. Text labels remain normal readable widgets.

## Contemporary drawn art, when that is the direction

Hand-designed does not mean aged paper, desaturated cottage colors, distressed edges or random wobbly strokes. For a clean contemporary brief, explore animation-cel shapes, lively editorial drawing, graphic toy worlds or controlled comic ink. Choose a purposeful contour rhythm, confident large color relationships, asymmetric poses and a few distinctive props. Keep edges and typography crisp. Texture is optional; do not use it to imply authorship when the silhouette and composition remain generic.

Develop the main subject, one useful environmental object and a control family together before filling out the screen. The subject may be a creature, puzzle or map; do not add a pet or room to a character-free brief. Compare them in one phone-sized composition: do their curve rhythm, contour emphasis, contact shadows and highlights belong together? A specific drawn icon, panel edge or readable button silhouette can carry that identity without making every object the same shape. If a bold illustrated subject sits against schematic scenery and unrelated stock controls, reauthor the weaker family; a shared accent color is insufficient. Preserve generous stable hit regions, legible labels and coherent press depth. More outlines and saturation alone do not establish design.

When the brief is open, compare genuinely different phone compositions using real type and the intended art before committing to components. Two creature silhouettes or backdrops inside the same header → centered hero → action stack are scene variants, not different compositions. Change a meaningful relationship: where the useful action lives, how the preview is framed, or how controls join the subject. Choose by useful action, character and hierarchy rather than novelty alone. A creature's pose, negative spaces and prop relationship should carry character before adding a face or accessories. Preserve a requested front-facing care layout when it serves the brief. Learn shape economy and graphic confidence from the atlas's clean illustrated references while authoring a new identity; do not reproduce their characters or brand palette.

## Allocate visual emphasis across the whole screen

Choose which subject and action deserve the strongest contrast. Related style does not mean identical emphasis: a character, wall seam, tiny status meter and primary button need different optical weight. Use contour strength, fill contrast, detail density and depth deliberately. A dark outlined cartoon can be excellent; the failure is making every architectural edge and control compete with the face. Keep supporting scenery authored and believable rather than schematic filler, but let it recede.

Treat frame surfaces, scene color, actions and selected state as separate palette roles. Warmth can come from the character and a few props; neutral surfaces need not inherit a yellow/cream wash. When the brief asks to avoid yellow dominance, compare large frame/sheet surfaces against an actual neutral and inspect home, choices, pressed/result and intermediate states together. Keep yellow as a purposeful accent where it helps. Do not enforce a universal hue ban or percentage target, and do not confuse an app-store marketing surround with the app itself.

Where a workflow has one next useful action, make it easier to find than secondary actions. Equal peer care actions can remain peers; they still need not all use maximum saturation, border weight and depth. In pickers, let the chosen object occupy enough of its tile to be recognized and compared; a tiny food icon in a large empty card wastes the useful surface. The home toolbar and a visual object chooser have different jobs; do not reuse their tile proportions automatically. During live styling or manipulation, preserve a readable preview and show selection on the changed object as well as its control. Use an appropriate anchored surface or scrim treatment without sacrificing modal clarity.

Give type its own hierarchy: expressive titles, calmer utility labels and readable state text can share a family without sharing the heaviest weight. Remove ornamental badges when they compete with the useful action. Inspect optical spacing, icon proportions and line breaks at actual phone size rather than relying on nominal equal sizes.

## Give the screen a small, legible loop

Set the current state, useful choice and visible consequence close together. On a pet screen: character + a few legible needs + care actions + a clear response. On a collection/map: current destination + readable progress + one next action. Do not surround the scene with promotional headings, redundant captions or placeholder stats. Keep labels that distinguish actions, selection or recovery. Render the same recognizable prop shape/color in a picker and in the world; a generic leaf icon should not stand for a peach that appears beside the mouth.

Make actions optically balanced and thumb-reachable. Use a clear pressed face, coherent shadow/contact behavior, and a result that changes the relevant art/state. For a beveled button, reserve the base depth within its stationary bounds and move only the face into that space; a base hidden behind the face is not a visible lower edge. Use motion to convey weight, attention and emotion: small anticipation, a readable response, then rest. Make the body participate when the verb calls for it: reach toward food, lean into play, settle into rest. When the action implies contact, place the hand, prop and mouth in one consistent pose space; independent transforms can leave the food floating beside an unrelated hand. Changing only a mouth, overlay or caption leaves the same static cutout. A modest authored pose change is more useful than unrelated sparkles. Keep persistent needs/selection separate from short reaction state. Capture a response frame as well as its settled state; confirm the reaction actually runs, rather than merely documenting it. A changed endpoint screenshot and an animated button do not establish animated character performance. Test a second action before the first reaction ends, and record how it resolves. Avoid a constant bounce on everything. Give different actions distinct expressions or props without building real audio or game systems for a visual prototype.

For compact choice sheets, preserve room context, use a clear selected treatment, keep dismissal predictable and fit controls above safe insets. At large text, permit a taller/scrolling sheet rather than clipping labels or reducing text. Test the smallest phone before polishing the largest presentation image. Also inspect tall screens for dead bands between the stage and controls. Let the stage, breathing room and reachable controls absorb height intentionally; a fixed square scene plus a bottom bar can leave an accidental void. Do not solve it with filler captions or decorative cards.

## Compare what is actually visible

Inspect primary examples from the mobile/game atlas at comparable viewport sizes. Ask: does the creature have an intentional silhouette and pose? Is habitat depth more convincing? Are the icon drawings and shadows consistent? Is the first action clearer? Are small labels and enlarged text readable? Does pressing change the local object rather than a distant toast? Name the largest visible gap and improve that material or composition first. More references or compliments do not establish comparable craft.
